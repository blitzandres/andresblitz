Bitcoin: days spent at each price

In plain English: Bitcoin has 4,439 daily closes on record since September 17, 2014, and this page sorts every one of them into US-dollar price ranges and counts the days in each. That shows where the price has actually spent its time: 44.4% of those days closed below $10,000, and 134 closed in the $70k–$80k range where the last close sits. It is a record of the past only, with no forecasts.

How many days has Bitcoin spent at each price? This chart splits the BTC/USD price into equal brackets and counts the days Bitcoin closed inside each one, from September 17, 2014. Pick a bracket size of $2,000, $3,000, $4,000, $5,000, $10,000 or $15,000. Today counts at the live price, so its bar can move while the day is still open. Prices are in US dollars and the chart can be used from any country.

History starts September 17, 2014 and runs through November 11, 2026 (4,439 completed daily closes). With $10,000 brackets, Bitcoin closed most often between $0 and $10k (1,972 days). Highest close $124,752.53 on October 6, 2025. History baked Nov 12, 2026, 2:10 a.m. PT.

How to read the Bitcoin price range days

Each bar is a price range in US dollars, for example $70,000 to $80,000. A longer bar means Bitcoin finished more days inside that range since 2014. Lower prices sit at the bottom. Narrow brackets ($2,000) show detail; wide brackets ($15,000) show the big picture. Each bar is softly color-coded by Bitcoin’s halving periods; a key under the chart names each period, and hovering or tapping one highlights it across every bracket. Today’s bracket is marked with a deeper-orange outline and a “← now” label, counted at the live price until the UTC day closes. This bitcoin price history is in USD and is the same worldwide.

Where the numbers come from

  • Completed days use Yahoo Finance’s BTC-USD daily close, from September 17, 2014 onward. A scheduled GitHub Action stores each new close in /bitcoin/history.json and pre-renders the table above.
  • The live price for today is CoinGecko’s BTC/USD print, checked about once a minute. If CoinGecko rate-limits, Coinbase’s public BTC-USD spot price is used instead and named as the source.
  • If a request fails, the last good data stays on screen with its time. Nothing is estimated.

FAQ

What does this chart show?

Each bar is a US dollar price bracket, for example $70,000 to $80,000. Its length is the number of days Bitcoin closed inside that bracket since September 17, 2014. Use the slider to switch between $2,000, $3,000, $4,000, $5,000, $10,000 and $15,000 bitcoin price history brackets.

How is a day counted?

A completed day counts once, in the bracket that contains that day’s Yahoo Finance BTC-USD close. Days follow the Yahoo daily series. Times on the page are shown in PT.

Why does the current bar move?

Today is not finished yet, so it is counted with the live Bitcoin price instead of a close. The page checks the price about once a minute, and if it crosses into another bracket, today’s day moves to that bar. Today’s bracket is marked with a deeper-orange outline and a “← now” label until the day closes. The bar itself stays color-coded by halving period.

Where does the data come from?

Daily closes come from Yahoo Finance’s public BTC-USD series, starting September 17, 2014 (the first day of that series). The live price for the unfinished day comes from CoinGecko’s free public API; if CoinGecko is rate-limiting, it falls back to Coinbase’s public BTC-USD spot price, and the page names whichever live source is shown.

Why does the history start when it does?

Yahoo Finance’s public BTC-USD daily series begins on September 17, 2014. That is why the chart starts there and can show time spent from $0 up. Earlier years are not included; Bitcoin traded below $10,000 throughout that earlier period.

Is this trading advice?

No. It is a historical reference chart only. There are no buy or sell buttons, signals, predictions or affiliate links.

Can I use this chart from any country?

Yes. It uses the global BTC/USD market price in US dollars, not a local-currency quote, so it is the same for visitors in any country. Times are shown in Pacific Time (PT) so everyone sees the same clock.
